# Pārvatī

Names searched: पार्वती, उमा, गौरी, अम्बिका, शिवा, हिमवत्पुत्री / हिमवत्तनया, शैलकन्या, गिरिजा, भवानी, अन्नपूर्णा, मीनाक्षी, कामाक्षी.

The common short “hello” people say in front of a goddess mūrti is **सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये… नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते** — that is a Durgā / Devī Māhātmya verse (Nārāyaṇī stuti), not a Pārvatī-only greeting. Gaurī paddhati itself borrows it. Do not silently reuse it here; it belongs with Durgā / Devī. Lead below is the one short Sanskrit recitation that names Pārvatī and stands alone.

## Proposal

Devanagari:

```
अन्नपूर्णे सदापूर्णे शङ्करप्राणवल्लभे ।
ज्ञानवैराग्यसिद्ध्यर्थं भिक्षां देहि च पार्वति ॥
```

IAST:

```
annapūrṇe sadāpūrṇe śaṅkaraprāṇavallabhe |
jñānavairāgyasiddhyarthaṃ bhikṣāṃ dehi ca pārvati ||
```

Padaccheda: not attested in the prints below; proposed: अन्नपूर्णे सदा-पूर्णे शङ्कर-प्राण-वल्लभे । ज्ञान-वैराग्य-सिद्ध्यर्थं भिक्षां देहि च पार्वति ॥

Word gloss:
- अन्नपूर्णे — O Annapūrṇā (she who is full of food)
- सदापूर्णे — O ever-full one
- शङ्करप्राणवल्लभे — O one dear as life to Śaṅkara
- ज्ञानवैराग्यसिद्ध्यर्थम् — for the accomplishment of knowledge and dispassion
- भिक्षाम् — alms
- देहि — give
- च — and
- पार्वति — O Pārvatī (vocative)

Translation: “O Annapūrṇā, ever complete, dear as life to Śaṅkara — give me alms so that knowledge and dispassion may be accomplished, O Pārvatī.”

Function: prārthanā (bhikṣā). Living use is kitchen / before meals / Kāśī Annapūrṇā, not a temple-walk namaskāra to a generic Pārvatī mūrti.

## Claims

(a) exists: yes — verse 11 of Śaṅkara’s Annapūrṇāstuti / Annapūrṇāstotram
(b) traditionally used: yes — Śṛṅgeri print, Smārta meal prayer, Kāśī Annapūrṇā
(c) standard short prayer: unclear — yes as the Annapūrṇā / food verse that names Pārvatī; no as the common hello to Pārvatī-as-such (that hello is the Durgā verse, or just ॐ पार्वत्यै नमः)
Confidence in (c): medium

## Sources

Hindu sources only. Count: 5 (strong as a circulating verse; pinned as Śaṅkara-stotra v. 11, not as a Purāṇa śloka)

1. Śṛṅgeri Śāradā Pīṭham, अन्नपूर्णास्तुतिः, verse 11 — https://www.srisharadapeetham.com/stotras/devistotrani/annapurnastutih — same recension (guru-paramparā)
2. Sanskrit Documents, Śrī Annapūrṇāstotram / Annapūrṇāṣṭakam, verse 11 (Complete Works of Śaṅkarācārya) — https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_devii/annapurna.html — same recension
3. Stotra Nidhi, Śrī Annapūrṇā stotram, verse 11 (also in their Prabhāta Stotranidhi book) — https://stotranidhi.com/en/annapurna-stotram-in-english/ — same recension
4. Kāśī Annapūrṇā temple pages that print this as the shrine verse (e.g. mandnam.com on the Kāśī temple) — same four pādas as daily temple / bhikṣā prayer
5. Indian-language prayer books: Śrī Mandir अन्नपूर्णा स्तोत्र; Bhakti Nidhi Hindi stotra — same verse 11 as the independently recited closing

## Variants

Verse 11 itself is stable across Śṛṅgeri, Sanskrit Documents, and Stotra Nidhi. Do not splice from the long preceding aṣṭaka (those verses have real recension drift: पावनकरी / पापनिकरी; मातान्नपूर्णेश्वरी sandhi).

Often paired, not mixed into this śloka, with verse 12:

- Śṛṅgeri / Stotra Nidhi: `माता च पार्वती देवी पिता देवो महेश्वरः । बान्धवाः शिवभक्ताश्च स्वदेशो भुवनत्रयम् ॥`
- Sanskrit Documents: `माता मे पार्वती देवी…` (मे vs च)

Web copies sometimes break sandhi (`शंकरः प्राणवल्लभे`) — ignore those.

## Other candidates

- **सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये शिवे सर्वार्थसाधिके । शरण्ये त्र्यम्बके गौरि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥** — this is the verse people actually say. It is Devī Māhātmya / Durgā Saptaśatī adhyāya 11 (Nārāyaṇī stuti). It does call her Gaurī and Śivā. Śrī Gaurī ṣoḍaśopacāra pūjā (HinduNidhi) uses it as prārthanā, and uses `नमो देव्यै महादेव्यै शिवायै सततं नमः` and `या देवी सर्वभूतेषु मातृरूपेण संस्थिता` as dhyāna / namaskāra — those are also Durgā Saptaśatī. Genuinely how paddhati greets Gaurī; still a Durgā hymn. Leave it for the Durgā / Devī row.
- **Tulasīdāsa, Rāmcaritmānas Bālakāṇḍa, Sītā’s Gaurī vandana** (`जय जय गिरिवर राज किशोरी…`) — this *is* a traditional Pārvatī greeting, especially for marriage / Gaurī pūjā. Awadhi, not a Sanskrit śloka. The circulating Sanskrit couplet `हे गौरी शङ्करार्धाङ्गी यथा त्वं शङ्करप्रिया । तथा मां कुरु कल्याणि कान्तकान्तां सुदुर्लभाम् ॥` is sold as “from Rāmcaritmānas”; the mūla there is Awadhi. Treat the Sanskrit as a later wedding mantra unless a paddhati pins it.
- **माता (मे/च) पार्वती देवी पिता देवो महेश्वरः…** — same stotra, verse 12. Independently known; it is a declaration of identity, not a greeting *to* her.
- **Śaṅkara, Gaurīdaśakam** (`गौरीमम्बामम्बुरुहाक्षीमहमीडे` refrain) — Śṛṅgeri and Sanskrit Documents; ten verses plus phalaśruti. Traditional Gaurī stuti, wrong size.
- **Śivarahasya, Devaiḥ kṛtā Gaurī stuti** opening `जय गौरि जगद्वन्द्ये जय शङ्करवल्लभे` — real Purāṇic-style stuti, twenty-plus verses, not a four-pāda hello.
- **Umā-Maheśvara stotra** (`नमो नमः शङ्करपार्वतीभ्याम्`) — Śṛṅgeri; joint Śiva–Pārvatī, not her alone. `कर्पूरगौरं… भवं भवानीसहितं नमामि` is the same problem and is already the Śiva proposal.
- **Kālidāsa, Raghuvamśa 1.1** `वागर्थाविव सम्पृक्तौ… जगतः पितरौ वन्दे पार्वतीपरमेश्वरौ` — pāṭhaśālā maṅgala, joint couple.
- **Gaurī pūjā āvāhana** `हेमाद्रितनयां देवीं वरदां शङ्करप्रियाम् । लम्बोदरस्य जननीं गौरीमावाहयाम्यहम् ॥` — Pārvatī-specific, right length, in the HinduNidhi Gaurī ṣoḍaśopacāra. Function is āvāhana in pūjā, not an independently memorized hello. Only one paddhati copy found.
- **वन्दे पार्वतीं देवीं हिमवत्तनयां शिवाम्…** — modern stotra-site dhyāna; no Purāṇa / pīṭha / paddhati pin. Ignore until a Hindu book cites it.
- Long forms not proposed: Gaurī / Pārvatī aṣṭakam and stotras, Mīnākṣī pañcaratna / stotra, Kāmākṣī stotra, Bhavānyāṣṭakam (`न तातो न माता… त्वमेका भवानि` — eight verses, and Bhavānī is often Durgā), Annapūrṇā verses 1–10.

## Uncertainties

- No short standalone Sanskrit namaskāra turned up that is *only* Pārvatī and is the village/school hello the way वक्रतुण्ड is for Gaṇeśa. The living hello is either the Durgā verse or a nāma-mantra (`ॐ पार्वत्यै नमः` / `ॐ उमामहेश्वराभ्यां नमः`).
- Leading with Annapūrṇā treats a well-known form (Kāśī, food, Śaṅkara) as the Pārvatī chapter. A family that greets Pārvatī at a Śiva-Pārvatī shrine may not say this.
- Śaṅkara ascription of the full stuti is traditional (Śṛṅgeri, collected works); not independently verified here verse-by-verse against a critical edition.
- Verse 12 मे vs च if that couplet is ever added.
- South Indian temple greetings to Mīnākṣī / Kāmākṣī are their own stotras, not a generic Pārvatī four-pāda.
