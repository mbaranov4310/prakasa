# Indra

## Proposal

Devanagari:

ऐरावतगजारूढं स्वर्णवर्णं किरीटिनम् ।
सहस्रनयनं शक्रं वज्रपाणिं विभावयेत् ॥

IAST:

airāvatagajārūḍhaṃ svarṇavarṇaṃ kirīṭinam |
sahasranayanaṃ śakraṃ vajrapāṇiṃ vibhāvayet ||

Padaccheda: no traditional padapāṭha found. The aṣṭadikpālaka print and Agni-purāṇa adhivāsana recite it as one anuṣṭubh. Proposed grammatical split: ऐरावत-गज-आरूढम् स्वर्ण-वर्णम् किरीटिनम् । सहस्र-नयनम् शक्रम् वज्र-पाणिम् विभावयेत् ॥

Word gloss:
- ऐरावतगजारूढम् (ऐरावत + गज + आरूढम्) — mounted on the elephant Airāvata
- स्वर्णवर्णम् (स्वर्ण + वर्णम्) — golden-coloured (accusative)
- किरीटिनम् — wearing a diadem / crown (accusative)
- सहस्रनयनम् (सहस्र + नयनम्) — thousand-eyed
- शक्रम् — Śakra (Indra; accusative)
- वज्रपाणिम् (वज्र + पाणिम्) — thunderbolt in hand
- विभावयेत् — one should visualize / meditate upon (optative of वि-भू)

Translation: “One should visualize Śakra: mounted on the elephant Airāvata, golden-hued, crowned, thousand-eyed, vajra in hand.”

Function: dhyānaśloka (east dikpāla in Agni-purāṇa liṅga-adhivāsana; verse 1 of the circulating अष्टदिक्पालकस्तोत्रम्). Not a child’s hello.

## Claims

(a) exists: yes — Agni-purāṇa 96.26, and the same four pādas as Indra’s verse in aṣṭadikpālaka prints
(b) traditionally used: yes, narrowly — priest’s visualization of the eastern lokapāla on the kalasha during adhivāsana / dikpāla recitation; not shown as a pāṭhaśālā / school / temple-walk greeting
(c) standard short prayer: no — there is no vakratuṇḍa-grade independently memorized hello for this deity
Confidence in (c): low

## Variants

Do not splice.

- `स्वर्णवर्णं किरीटिनम्` (lead; aṣṭadikpālaka stotra) vs Agni-purāṇa dumps `स्वर्णवण किरीटिनं` (TransLiteral, ptvaishnavi Sanskrit). The Hindi anuvāda (“सुवर्ण के समान”) shows वर्ण is intended. Keep `वर्णं` in the lead; do not encode the broken *vaṇa*.
- `किरीटिनम्` vs `किरीटिनं` (anusvāra vs *m*)
- `सहस्रनयनं` (96.26 and the stotra) vs `सहस्त्रनयनं` (96.25’s truncated second pāda in the same adhyāya — a different, incomplete line; do not mix)
- Optional label `श्री इन्द्रस्तुतिः – पूर्व` in stotra prints; not part of the verse
- Living dikpāla *āvāhana* is a different text (`इन्द्रं सुरपतिश्रेष्ठं…`); see Other candidates. Not a variant of this dhyāna.

## Other candidates

- `नमस्ते देवदेवेश नमस्ते बलसूदन । नमुचिघ्न नमस्तेऽस्तु सहस्राक्ष शचीपते ॥` — genuine namaskāra, vakratuṇḍa-shaped, Mahābhārata Ādi 21.7 (कद्रुकृतं इन्द्रस्तोत्रम्, Sanskrit Documents). Opening of an 11-verse narrative stotra (Kadrū asking Indra for rain for the snakes). Not isolated in bāla-pāṭha. If the human wants greeting-*shape* over paddhati dhyāna, this is the cleaner namaskāra.
- `वज्रस्य भर्ता भुवनस्य गोप्ता वृत्रस्य हन्ता नमुचेर्निहन्ता । … नमः सदास्मै जगदीश्वराय लोकत्रयेशाय पुरन्दराय ॥` — Mahābhārata Ādi 3.152–153 (Pauṣya; extracted on Sanskrit Documents as इन्द्रस्तोत्रम्). Two triṣṭubh verses, real namaskāra, too long and not a children’s hello.
- `इन्द्रं सुरपतिश्रेष्ठं वज्रहस्तं महाबलम् । आवाहये यज्ञसिद्धयै शतयज्ञाधिपं प्रभुम् ॥` — daśadikpāla āvāhana in TransLiteral nitya-karma / dikpāla-sthāpanā paddhati (and the same site’s दश दिक्पाल-पूजन). Recited after Ṛgveda 6.47.11, then `इन्द्रमावाहयामि, स्थापयामि`. Living purohita use; wrong job (āvāhana inside a ten-deity sequence, not a greeting). One paddhati lineage, copies on blogs; not a third independent recension.
- `ॐ त्रातारमिन्द्रमवितारमिन्द्रं हवेहवे सुहवं शूरमिन्द्रम् । ह्वयामि शक्रं पुरुहूतमिन्द्रं स्वस्ति नो मघवा धात्विन्द्रः ॥` — Ṛgveda 6.47.11, used as the Vedic āvāhana ṛk in that same dikpāla paddhati. Independently used in yajña, not as a daily maṅgala-śloka. Out of this book’s yardstick.
- `देवराजाय विद्महे वज्रहस्ताय धीमहि । तन्नः शक्रः प्रचोदयात् ॥` — Liṅga-purāṇa uttara 48.18 (pratiṣṭhā gāyatrī list); also in nānāvidha-gāyatrī dumps. Popular print `ॐ सहस्रनेत्राय विद्महे वज्रहस्ताय धीमहि । तन्नो इन्द्रः प्रचोदयात् ॥` (Vikatan Tamil Indra pūjā uses a gāyatrī of this type with `ॐ இந்திராய நம` and a 21-nāmāvali). Gāyatrī / japa, not a greeting śloka.
- `पुरन्दरामरपते वज्रपाणे शतक्रतो । प्रगृह्यतां बलिर्देव विधिमन्त्रपुरस्कृतः ॥` — Nāṭyaśāstra 3, bali-mantra to Indra before a performance (TransLiteral Nāṭyaśāstra adhyāya 3). Real Hindu text; stage offering, not a home/school hello.
- `नमामीन्द्रं सुरेश्वरम्` aṣṭaka opening `वज्रपाणिं सहस्राक्षं देवराजं पुरन्दरम् । ऐरावतगजारूढं नमामीन्द्रं सुरेश्वरम् ॥` — only traced on VastuCart. Same pattern as that site’s untraced Brahmā aṣṭaka. Do not crown.
- Srimandir “इंद्र स्तुति” (`त्वं हि देवाधिराजोऽसि…`) — modern compilation, not a mūla.
- `ॐ इन्द्राय नमः` — the actual short formula in pūjā and Tamil Indra worship; not a śloka.
- Ṛgveda Indra sūktas (`स जनास इन्द्रः` 2.12; `इन्द्रं वो विश्वतस्परि…`) and “Vedic Indra stotram” anthologies — Vedic; out unless a specific ṛk is the daily hello. None is.
- Kadrū stotra as a whole (Ādi 21.7–17); Nārada-kṛta Indra stotra (Udyoga 16.14–17); Indra-sahasranāma (Gaṇapati Muni) — real, too long.
- Indra-*kṛta* stotras to Kṛṣṇa, Rāma, Lakṣmī, Śiva — Indra as *author*, wrong direction.

Could not find a short standalone greeting in bāla-pāṭha, nitya-prārthanā, or school readers under the names above, nor a Tamil / Telugu / Kannada / Malayalam / Marathi / Bengali / Gujarati children’s hello.

## Uncertainties

- This *is* a dikpāla dhyāna (and aṣṭadikpālaka verse 1). (c) fails: children and nitya-pārāyaṇa lists do not isolate it as “the Indra hello.” If the human wants only a verse that is independently the greeting, the result is **cannot find one**.
- The aṣṭadikpālaka stotra on Sanskrit Documents looks extracted from Agni-purāṇa 96’s lokapāla dhyānas (Indra’s verse matches 96.26; Agni’s matches 96.27). That is Hindu packaging, not a second mūla. Two prints of one Purāṇa plus one extraction.
- Padaccheda is proposed from grammar, not from a traditional padapāṭha.
- `स्वर्णवण` in some AP dumps is a broken reading of `स्वर्णवर्णं`.
- Indra’s living household presence is as east dikpāla and yajña-devatā (`ॐ इन्द्राय नमः`, gāyatrī, Vedic āvāhana), not as a mūrti people greet on a temple walk. That is evidence against (c), not a licence to invent a school verse.
