# Vāyu

## Proposal

Could not find a short standalone greeting of this book’s shape.

Yardstick is Gaṇeśa (`वक्रतुण्ड महाकाय…`): 1–2 anuṣṭubh ślokas, independently memorized as the hello. Names searched: वायु, पवन, अनिल, मारुत, वात, समीर, वायुदेव, मुख्यप्राण, मातरिश्वन्, पवमान, गन्धवाह; Tamil வாயு; Telugu వాయువు; Kannada ವಾಯು; Malayalam വായു; Hindi/Marathi पवनदेव. Queries: श्लोक, प्रार्थना, ध्यान श्लोक, नमस्कार, स्तुति, स्तोत्रम्, दैनिक प्रार्थना, and the same in those languages.

What turned up instead:

- Children’s / nitya-pārāyaṇa lists skip Vāyu. Stotra Nidhi बाल श्लोकाः and Vaidika Vignanam नित्य पारायण श्लोकाः greet Hanumān with `मनोजवं मारुततुल्यवेगं… वातात्मजं…` — already encoded for Hanumān. Do not steal it.
- The only widely attested short namaskāra *to* Vāyu is Vedic (Taittirīya śānti). This book is non-Vedic maṅgala-ślokas. Recorded below so it can be audited; not a chapter candidate unless a Vedic exception is chosen.
- Madhva daily recitation is the 41-verse Hari-Vāyu Stuti (too long; Mukhyaprāṇa as Hanumān–Bhīma–Madhva).
- Dikpāla āvāhana anuṣṭubhs exist inside karmakāṇḍa sequences; they are not a child’s hello.

Closest living namaskāra (Vedic; out of this book’s stated scope):

Devanagari:

नमो ब्रह्मणे । नमस्ते वायो ।
त्वमेव प्रत्यक्षं ब्रह्मासि ।
त्वामेव प्रत्यक्षं ब्रह्म वदिष्यामि ।

IAST:

namo brahmaṇe | namaste vāyo |
tvam eva pratyakṣaṃ brahmāsi |
tvām eva pratyakṣaṃ brahma vadiṣyāmi |

Padaccheda: the Upaniṣad already splits on daṇḍas. Traditional Vedic padapāṭha of this anuvāka exists; not a separate stotra-padapāṭha. Proposed from that split:

नमः ब्रह्मणे । नमः ते वायो । त्वम् एव प्रत्यक्षम् ब्रह्म असि । त्वाम् एव प्रत्यक्षम् ब्रह्म वदिष्यामि ।

Word gloss:
- नमः — salutation
- ब्रह्मणे — to Brahman (dative; traditional reading here: Vāyu as Brahman)
- नमस्ते (नमः + ते) — salutation to you
- वायो — O Vāyu (vocative)
- त्वम् एव — you alone
- प्रत्यक्षम् — perceptible / directly visible
- ब्रह्म — Brahman
- असि — you are
- त्वाम् एव — you alone (accusative)
- वदिष्यामि — I shall declare

Translation: “Salutation to Brahman. Salutation to you, O Vāyu. You alone are the perceptible Brahman. You alone I shall declare as the perceptible Brahman.”

Function: Vedic śānti namaskāra (Taittirīya Śikṣāvallī 1 / Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 7.1; also the close of Śikṣāvallī 12 in past tense). Opening of Vedic study, not a home/school maṅgala-śloka.

These four cola sit *inside* a longer anuvāka that also names Mitra, Varuṇa, Aryaman, Indra, Bṛhaspati, and Viṣṇu, then ṛta / satya and “protect me and the teacher.” Do not splice the Vāyu lines out as if they were a free-standing anuṣṭubh.

## Claims

(a) exists: yes for the Taittirīya namaskāra; **no** for a vakratuṇḍa-grade nitya-maṅgala greeting
(b) traditionally used: yes as Upaniṣad / Āraṇyaka śānti-pāṭha and Vedic-study opening; not shown as a pāṭhaśālā / school / temple-walk hello to Vāyu-deva
(c) standard short prayer: **no** — there is no independently memorized two-line hello for this deity
Confidence in (c): high (that (c) is no)

## Variants

Do not splice.

- Taittirīya opening vs close: anuvāka 1 future (`वदिष्यामि`, `अवतु`) vs anuvāka 12 past (`ब्रह्मावादिषम्`, `आवीत्`). Same namaskāra, two ritual positions. Do not mix tenses.
- `त्वामेव प्रत्यक्षं ब्रह्म वदिष्यामि` (Sanskrit Documents; expected accusative) vs Vignanam svara print `त्वमेव प्रत्यक्षं ब्रह्म वदिष्यामि` on the second colon — print/pāṭha difference, not two prayers.
- Full first anuvāka includes `शं नो मित्रः… विष्णुरुरुक्रमः` before the Vāyu address, and `ऋतं वदिष्यामि । सत्यं वदिष्यामि । तन्मामवतु…` after. Extracting only the Vāyu cola is an editorial cut, not a traditional standalone śloka.
- Optional `ॐ` as the śānti frame (`ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः`) — Vedic close, not a stotra prefix to splice onto a maṅgala verse.

## Other candidates

- `मनोजवं मारुततुल्यवेगं जितेन्द्रियं बुद्धिमतां वरिष्ठम् । वातात्मजं वानरयूथमुख्यं श्रीरामदूतं शरणं प्रपद्ये ॥` — the common short Sanskrit hello in this slot. It is Hanumān’s (Rāmarakṣā 33; already encoded). Names मारुत / वात because he is Vāyu’s son. Do not take it for Vāyu.
- Hari-Vāyu Stuti / खिलवायुस्तुतिः, Trivikrama Paṇḍitācārya, 41 sragdharā verses, framed by Madhva’s नखस्तुति (`पान्त्वस्मान्…`). Daily in Madhva homes and Udupi aṣṭamaṭhas (Stotra Nidhi, Vaidika Vignanam, Sanskrit Documents). Too long; praises Mukhyaprāṇa as Hanumān–Bhīma–Madhva, not a child’s namaskāra to elemental Vāyu.
- Laghu Vāyu Stuti (Kalyāṇī Devī), six verses, refrain `नन्दयामो वयं नन्दितीर्थम्` — shorter Madhva cousin; still six verses and avatāra-traya, not two lines.
- Śrīpādarāja, Kannada मध्वनाम — vernacular digest of the Vāyu Stuti for those who do not recite the Sanskrit; wrong language and length.
- `प्रथमो हनुमान् नामा द्वितीयो भीम एव च । पूर्णप्रज्ञः तृतीयस्तु भगवत्कार्यसाधकः ॥` — circulating Madhva identification anuṣṭubh (Madhva blogs / Avatāra-traya pages). Right length; function is theology (three avatāras), not a greeting to Vāyu. No pinned stotra / Purāṇa verse-number found. Do not crown.
- Dikpāla / pañcalokapāla āvāhana (one Hindi karmakāṇḍa lineage, “डी पी कर्मकांड”):
  - `आवाहयाम्यहं वायुं भूतानां देहधारिणम् । सर्वाधारं महावेगं मृगवाहनमीश्वरम् ॥`
  - `मनोजवं महातेजं सर्वतश्चारिणं शुभम् । यज्ञसंरक्षणार्थाय वायुमावाहयाम्यहम् ॥`
  Right metre; used as āvāhana inside a ten-deity sequence, not as a standalone hello. Two different recensions in the same series. The second *opens* `मनोजवं` — easy to confuse with Hanumān’s verse; it is not that verse. One blog lineage is not 2–3 independent sources. Prefaced by the Vedic `आ नो नियुद्भिः… वायो अस्मिन्त्सवने मादयस्व` (Ṛgveda 1.135.1-type dikpāla mantra).
- `ॐ वायवे नमः` / `ॐ वायुदेवाय नमः` — the actual short formula in dikpāla / nāma-japa. Mantra, not a śloka.
- Vāyu gāyatrī (Sanskrit Documents गायत्रीमन्त्राः list), two recensions: `ॐ सर्वप्राणाय विद्महे यष्टिहस्ताय धीमहि तन्नो वायुः प्रचोदयात् ॥` and `ॐ पवनपुरुषाय विद्महे सहस्रमूर्तये च धीमहि तन्नो वायुः प्रचोदयात् ॥` — mantra, not a maṅgala-śloka.
- Ṛgveda 1.2.1 `वायवा याहि दर्शतेमे सोमा अरंकृताः । तेषां पाहि श्रुधी हवम् ॥` — genuine Vāyu invocation (Hindu RV pāṭha: sanatana.in / sa.wikisource). Vedic yajña, not a greeting.
- Ṛgveda 10.186 (Vāta) `वात आ वातु भेषजं शम्भु मयोभु नो हृदे । प्र ण आयूंषि तारिषत् ॥` — three ṛks; TransLiteral Foundations prints the sūkta. Vedic healing hymn to Vāta, not a nitya hello.
- Pavamāna sūkta — Soma-filter hymn; वातः appears; not a Vāyu-deva greeting.
- Samartha Rāmadāsa, Marathi समास सहावा वायुस्तवन (`धन्य धन्य हा वायुदेव…`) — living vernacular; too long and wrong language.
- Stotra Nidhi श्री वायुनन्दनाष्टकम् (`वन्देऽहं वायुनन्दनम्`) — Hanumān as Vāyu’s son, not Vāyu.
- Pañcabhūta Kālahasti — Vāyu-*liṅga* of Śiva, not a Vāyu-deva pāṭha.
- VastuCart “Vāyu aṣṭakam” (`मातरिश्वानं महाभागं… वायुदेवं नमाम्यहम्`) and “dvādaśa nāma” — untraced outside that site; Vāyu-Purāṇa ascription not shown. Do not crown.

Could not find a short standalone greeting in bāla-pāṭha, nitya-prārthanā, or school readers under the names above, nor a Tamil / Telugu / Kannada / Malayalam / Hindi / Marathi children’s hello *to* Vāyu (as opposed to Hanumān / Mukhyaprāṇa’s long stuti).

## Uncertainties

- No Purāṇa / āgama verse-number for a free-standing Vāyu maṅgala-śloka. Strong living use is (1) Vedic śānti, (2) Madhva Hari-Vāyu Stuti, (3) ॐ वायवे नमः, (4) greeting the son (Hanumān). None of those is this book’s shape except (3), which is not a śloka.
- Traditional vyākhyā of `नमो ब्रह्मणे` in this anuvāka: both Śaṅkara-line and Madhva-line readings take Vāyu as the pratyakṣa Brahman addressed. Do not split ब्रह्मन् and वायु into two deities here.
- Sandhyāvandana: this Taittirīya śānti is the opening of Taittirīya study / some Kṛṣṇa-Yajur paddhatis. Śukla-Yajur sandhyā dumps checked here do not use it as the daily Vāyu hello. Do not claim “everyone’s sandhyā.”
- Madhva `भारतीरमणमुख्यप्राणान्तर्गत…` is a saṅkalpa formula, not a śloka.
- One dikpāla āvāhana starts `मनोजवं महातेजं` — different text from Hanumān’s `मनोजवं मारुततुल्यवेगं`. Record the clash; do not merge.
- Popular pages that dump a Vāyu aṣṭakam “from the Vāyu Purāṇa” were not matched to a printed Purāṇa chapter. Leave unproposed.
