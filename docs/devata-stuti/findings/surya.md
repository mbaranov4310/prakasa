# Sūrya

## Proposal

Devanagari:

जपाकुसुमसङ्काशं काश्यपेयं महाद्युतिम् ।
तमोऽरिं सर्वपापघ्नं प्रणतोऽस्मि दिवाकरम् ॥

IAST:

japākusumasaṅkāśaṃ kāśyapeyaṃ mahādyutim |
tamo'riṃ sarvapāpaghnaṃ praṇato'smi divākaram ||

Padaccheda: not attested as a traditional pada-pāṭha; proposed from the usual anvaya in Indian-language teaching:

जपाकुसुम-सङ्काशम् काश्यपेयम् महा-द्युतिम् तमः-अरिम् सर्व-पाप-घ्नम् प्रणतः अस्मि दिवाकरम्

Word gloss:
- जपाकुसुमसङ्काशम् — resembling the japā flower (red hibiscus; Hindi गुड़हल, Marathi जास्वंद)
- काश्यपेयम् — son / descendant of Kaśyapa
- महाद्युतिम् — of great brilliance
- तमोऽरिम् (तमः + अरिम्) — enemy of darkness
- सर्वपापघ्नम् — destroyer of all sins
- प्रणतः — bowed
- अस्मि — I am
- दिवाकरम् — to Divākara, the day-maker

Translation: “I bow to Divākara, who resembles the red japā flower, son of Kaśyapa, of great brilliance, enemy of darkness, destroyer of all sins.”

Function: namaskāra / dhyānaśloka (pādas 1–3 are dhyāna; pāda 4 is the bow). In paddhati the last pāda is sometimes swapped for āvāhana.

## Claims

(a) exists: yes — verse 1 of the व्यास-विरचित नवग्रहस्तोत्र; also printed as a standalone Sūrya पौराणिक मंत्र
(b) traditionally used: yes — graha-japa dhyāna, navagraha-maṇḍala / gṛhapraveśa āvāhana, janana-śānti; Vignanam files it under nitya / dainandika prārthanā
(c) standard short prayer: yes — the common one-śloka Sūrya hello, extracted and memorized on its own, not only as part of the nine-verse stotra
Confidence in (c): high

## Variants

- `संकाशं` (Sanskrit Documents) vs `सङ्काशं` (Wikisource, Stotra Nidhi, Vignanam) — same word
- `प्रणतोऽस्मि` vs `प्रणतोस्मि` (avagraha written or not)
- `तमोऽरिं` vs rarer `ध्वान्तारिं` (same sense: foe of darkness)
- `महाद्युतिम्` vs occasional `महद्युतिम्`
- Paddhati last pāda: `तमोऽरिं सर्वपापघ्नं सूर्य मावाहयाम्यहम्` (āvāhana; do not splice into the namaskāra recension)
- Some prints insert `ॐ` before the verse; some split `जपाकुसुम सङ्काशं` / `सर्व पापघ्नं` for reading, not a different text

Do not mix the namaskāra pāda with the āvāhana pāda.

## Other candidates

- `आदिदेव नमस्तुभ्यं प्रसीद मम भास्कर । दिवाकर नमस्तुभ्यं प्रभाकर नमोऽस्तु ते ॥` — Sūryāṣṭakam v. 1 (Sringeri Sharada Peetham; Sanskrit Documents with साम्ब उवाच; Tamil printings). Pure namaskāra, child-length. Traditional Sāmba Purāṇa / साम्ब उवाच attribution, no chapter-verse pin in sources used. Not led because it is the opening of an eight-verse stotra; japākusuma is the more widely extracted standalone hello.
- `सप्ताश्वरथमारूढं प्रचण्डं कश्यपात्मजम् । श्वेतपद्मधरं देवं तं सूर्यं प्रणमाम्यहम् ॥` — Sūryāṣṭakam v. 2; iconographic namaskāra, not the common one-liner.
- `ध्येयः सदा सवितृमण्डलमध्यवर्ती नारायणः सरसिजासनसन्निविष्टः । केयूरवान् मकरकुण्डलवान् किरीटी हारी हिरण्मयवपुर्धृतशङ्खचक्रः ॥` — Sūrya-Nārāyaṇa dhyāna in Vignanam सूर्य नित्य पूजा / सूर्य नमस्कार मन्त्रम् and Stotra Nidhi Sūrya namaskāra. Strong paddhati verse, but it meditates Viṣṇu in the solar orb, not Sūrya as the greeting-deity; denser than the Gaṇeśa yardstick.
- `पद्मासनः पद्मकरो द्विबाहुः पद्मद्युतिः सप्ततुरङ्गवाहः । दिवाकरो लोकगुरुः किरीटी मयि प्रसादं विदधातु देवः ॥` — Stotra Nidhi graha-japa dhyāna *alongside* japākusuma; temple-iconography, less the school-yard hello.
- `जपाकुसुमसंकाशं द्विभुजं पद्महस्तकम्…` — Āditya kavaca dhyāna (Vignanam); same incipit, different verse, longer.
- Ādityahṛdaya (Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Yuddhakāṇḍa) — too long. No 1–2 śloka excerpt turned up as an independent greeting.
- Gāyatrī (`तत्सवितुर्वरेण्यम्`) — different collection; not for this book.
- `ॐ सूर्याय नमः` / twelve names (`मित्राय… भास्कराय`) / `ॐ घृणिः सूर्य आदित्यः` — namaskāra formulas and arghya mantras, not a śloka of this kind.
- `नमोऽस्तु सूर्याय सहस्ररश्मये…` — inside Ādityahṛdaya, not independently the hello.

## Uncertainties

- No Purāṇa / āgama verse-number for japākusuma. Traditional ascription is Vyāsa (नवग्रहस्तोत्र); TransLiteral calls it पौराणिक. Do not invent a Purāṇa cite.
- (c) is high for the pan-Indian one-śloka Sūrya namaskāra. South Indian Sūrya-Nārāyaṇa ṣoḍaśopacāra (Vignanam) prefers `ध्येयः सदा…` as the pūjā dhyāna; `आदिदेव नमस्तुभ्यं` is a real rival as a pure Sūrya prārthanā.
- Padaccheda and glosses are proposed from circulating Hindu anvaya / Hindi-Marathi-Tamil meanings, not from a named bhāṣya.
- `काश्यपेय` is “Kaśyapa’s son” in every Indian-language gloss used here (Sūrya as Āditya, child of Aditi and Kaśyapa).
- Chhath/arghya blogs repeat this verse; treated as popularity, not a separate source.
