# Nāga

Lead is **generic nāga** (the nine-name greeting), not Ananta/Śeṣa alone and not Vāsuki alone. That is what people actually say as the short hello. Ananta and Śeṣa are two of the nine names in this recension; do not collapse them, and do not splice a Śeṣa-only or Vāsuki-only verse onto this list.

## Proposal

Devanagari:

अनन्तं वासुकिं शेषं पद्मनाभं च कम्बलम् ।
शङ्खपालं धृतराष्ट्रं तक्षकं कालियं तथा ॥

IAST:

anantaṃ vāsukiṃ śeṣaṃ padmanābhaṃ ca kambalam |
śaṅkhapālaṃ dhṛtarāṣṭraṃ takṣakaṃ kāliyaṃ tathā ||

Padaccheda: no traditional padapāṭha found. The line is nine accusatives plus च / तथा. Proposed:

अनन्तम् | वासुकिम् | शेषम् | पद्मनाभम् | च | कम्बलम् । शङ्खपालम् | धृतराष्ट्रम् | तक्षकम् | कालियम् | तथा ॥

Word gloss:
- अनन्तम् — Ananta (the endless one; accusative)
- वासुकिम् — Vāsuki (accusative)
- शेषम् — Śeṣa (accusative)
- पद्मनाभम् — Padmanābha (here a *nāga* name, not Viṣṇu; accusative)
- च — and
- कम्बलम् — Kambala (accusative)
- शङ्खपालम् — Śaṅkhapāla (accusative)
- धृतराष्ट्रम् — Dhṛtarāṣṭra (the nāga, not the Kuru king; accusative)
- तक्षकम् — Takṣaka (accusative)
- कालियम् — Kāliya (accusative)
- तथा — and / likewise

Translation: “Ananta, Vāsuki, Śeṣa, Padmanābha and Kambala; Śaṅkhapāla, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Takṣaka, and likewise Kāliya.” (Accusative nāma-smarana: circulating Hindu arthas supply “I remember / I salute” these nine.)

Function: nāma-smarana / nāma-stotra (the independently memorized Nāga Pañcamī and prātaḥ–sāyaṃ nāga greeting; not a vocative prārthanā of the vakratuṇḍa shape)

Traditional continuation (phala; not part of the lead line — do not splice extra pādas into the greeting). Short recension (Sanskrit Documents / TransLiteral stotra dump):

एतानि नवनामानि नागानां च महात्मनाम् ।
सायङ्काले पठेन्नित्यं प्रातःकाले विशेषतः ॥
तस्य विषभयं नास्ति सर्वत्र विजयी भवेत् ॥

“These are the nine names of the great-souled nāgas. One should recite them every evening, and especially in the morning. For him there is no fear of poison; he becomes victorious everywhere.”

## Claims

(a) exists: yes — printed as नवनागनामस्तोत्रम् / श्री नाग स्तोत्रम् (नवनाग स्तोत्रम्), verse 1
(b) traditionally used: yes — Nāga Pañcamī pāṭha; Marathi nityopāsanā prātaḥsmaraṇa among other school-length ślokas; printed in Stotra Nidhi’s Subrahmaṇya pārāyaṇa grantha as a *nāga* stotra (South-Indian nāga–Subrahmaṇya association; not the Kārtikeya dhyāna)
(c) standard short prayer: yes — the common two-line Sanskrit nāga hello when a śloka is used; the living act is often milk at an anthill / `ॐ नागराजाय नमः`, not a daily Gaṇeśa-shaped greeting
Confidence in (c): medium

## Variants

Do not splice.

- Lead recension: `पद्मनाभं च कम्बलम्` / `धृतराष्ट्रं` / `कालियं` (Sanskrit Documents, Stotra Nidhi, Vignanam, TransLiteral)
- `नवनामानि` (one compound; Sanskrit Documents) vs `नव नामानि` (Stotra Nidhi, Vignanam, prātaḥsmaraṇa)
- `धृतराष्ट्रं` (lead) vs occasional `धार्तराष्ट्रं` (same nāga; do not mix into the lead line)
- `कालियं` (lead) vs paddhati `कालीयं`
- Phala length: short recension stops after `तस्य विषभयं नास्ति सर्वत्र विजयी भवेत्` (Sanskrit Documents; TransLiteral stotra page). Longer recension (Stotra Nidhi, Vignanam) inserts two extra anuṣṭubhs before that phala-pāda: `सन्तानं प्राप्यते नूनं सन्तानस्य च रक्षकाः । सर्वबाधा विनिर्मुक्तः सर्वत्र विजयी भवेत् ॥` and `सर्पदर्शनकाले वा पूजाकाले च यः पठेत् । तस्य विषभयं नास्ति सर्वत्र विजयी भवेत् ॥` then `ॐ नागराजाय नमः प्रार्थयामि नमस्करोमि`. Same greeting line; extra phala only.
- Optional `श्रीगणेशाय नमः` before the names in some prints
- Bhaviṣya / ṣoḍaśopacāra cousin lists `पद्मं कम्बलमेव च` and add कार्कोटक, भुजङ्ग, अश्वतर, पिङ्गल — a different text, not a variant of verse 1

## Other candidates

- `नमस्करोमि देवेश नागेन्द्र हरभूषण । अभीष्टदायिने तुभ्यं अहिराज नमो नमः ॥` — dhyāna / namaskāra before श्रीनागराजाष्टोत्तरशतनामावलिः (Sanskrit Documents). True two-line hello to Nāgarāja as Hara’s ornament. Not led: it opens a 108-name list; far less independently memorized than अनन्तं वासुकिं.
- `अनन्ताय नमस्तुभ्यं सहस्रशिरसे नमः । नमोऽस्तु पद्मनाभाय नागानां पतये नमः ॥` — dhyāna of श्री अनन्तपद्मनाभाष्टोत्तरशतनामावलिः (Sanskrit Documents). Ananta-named, but the aṣṭottara mixes Śeṣa-epithets with Viṣṇu (gadādhara, catur-bāhu, śaṅkhacakradhara) — Ananta-Padmanābha of the Tiruvanantapuram / Ananta-caturdaśī track, not the nāga-as-nāga hello.
- `नागराज नमस्तुभ्यं देवेश हरभूषण । विष्णुतल्प नमस्तेऽस्तु सर्पभीतिं निवारय ॥` — Cidambaram temple-deity stotra, verse for श्रीनागराजः (Sanskrit Documents, चिदम्बरेश्वर–नटराज देवालयस्थ देवतास्तोत्रम्). Compact Ananta/Śeṣa namaskāra (both Hara-bhūṣaṇa and Viṣṇu-talpa). Temple-set excerpt, not the pan-Indian short prayer.
- `ब्रह्माण्डाधारभूतं च भुवनान्तरवासिनम् । फणयुक्तमहं ध्याये नागराजं हरिप्रियम् ॥` — third śloka of the Stotra Nidhi नाग पञ्चमी पूजा पद्धतिः dhyāna. Ananta/Śeṣa as Nāgarāja, Hari’s couch. Lives inside a three-verse 12-nāga dhyāna (`अनन्तं वासुकिं शेषं पद्मकम्बलकौ तथा… पिङ्गलं च महानागं सपत्नीकान्प्रपूजयेत्`), not extracted as the hello.
- `आगच्छानन्त देवेश काल पन्नगनायक । अनन्तशयनीयं त्वां भक्त्या ह्यावाहयाम्यहम् ॥` — same paddhati, āvāhana to Ananta. Pūjā formula, not a standalone greeting.
- `नमस्ते सर्वलोकेश नमस्ते लोकवन्दित । नमस्तेऽस्तु सदा नाग त्राहि मां दुःखसागरात् ॥` — same paddhati, namaskāra-upacāra. Genuine two-line prārthanā to Nāga; buried in ṣoḍaśopacāra, not the memorized hello.
- Bhaviṣya Purāṇa Uttara 36.30–31 (Nāga Pañcamī vrata; TransLiteral dump, OCR messy): namaskāra to nāgas on earth, Himālaya, mid-space, heaven, rivers, lakes, wells. Real Purāṇa festival text; not the children’s two-liner.
- `अनन्तो नागराजेन्द्रः शिवपादार्चने रतः । महापापविषं हत्वा शान्तिमाशु करोतु मे ॥` — verse 2 of नागशान्तिस्तोत्रम्, from Śivadharma upapurāṇa Śāntikādhyāya (Sanskrit Documents). Ananta-specific, with a mūla. Twenty-one verses covering eight nāgas; śānti, not the hello.
- `ब्रह्मलोके च ये सर्पाः शेषनाग पुरोगमाः । नमोऽस्तु तेभ्यः सुप्रीताः प्रसन्नाः सन्तु मे सदा ॥` — सर्प स्तोत्रम् v. 1 (Stotra Nidhi / Vignanam). Twelve-verse realm-by-realm namaskāra (Śeṣa in Brahmaloka, Vāsuki in Viṣṇuloka, Takṣaka in Rudraloka…). Used on Nāga Pañcamī in those collections; too long, and not one deity’s greeting.
- `श्रीमद्विष्णुपदाम्भोज पीठायुत फणातलम् । शेषत्वैक स्वरूपं तं आदिशेषमुपास्महे ॥` — श्री आदिशेष स्तवम् v. 1 (Stotra Nidhi). Real Ādiśeṣa upāsanā (Telugu/Kannada prints); ~30 verses. The Ananta/Śeṣa *stotra*, not the short hello.
- Śāṇḍilya-saṃhitā श्रीशेषस्तोत्रम् (Sanskrit Documents) — long; refrain तमीशं फणीशं गुरुं शेषमीडे. Not a 1–2 line greeting.
- Bhāgavata 6.16 Citaketu’s Ananta-stotra (Sanskrit Documents चित्रकेतुकृतम् अनन्तस्तोत्रम्) — Purāṇa mūla, too long.
- `ॐ नागराजाय विद्महे सर्पराजाय धीमहि । तन्नो नागः प्रचोदयात् ॥` and cousins (`ॐ अनन्ताय विद्महे… तन्नो शेषः प्रचोदयात्`, `ॐ नवकुलाय विद्महे विषदन्ताय धीमहि तन्नो सर्पः प्रचोदयात्`, सर्पसूक्तम्’s `भुजङ्गेशाय विद्महे…`) — Gāyatrī / Veda-shaped mantra, not a śloka-greeting. सर्पसूक्तम् itself is Vedic (Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.2.8 in Stotra Nidhi); out of this book’s yardstick.
- `ॐ नागराजाय नमः` / `ॐ अनन्ताय नमः` / `ॐ शेषाय नमः` / `ॐ वासुकये नमः` — living nāma; not a śloka.
- `शान्ताकारं भुजगशयनं पद्मनाभं सुरेशं… वन्दे विष्णुं…` — Viṣṇu dhyāna (Anantaśayana). Do not steal for Nāga.
- Kārtikeya `षडाननं कुङ्कुमरक्तवर्णं… गुहं सदा शरणमहं प्रपद्ये` — already encoded; South-Indian nāga–Subrahmaṇya overlap does not make it a nāga verse. This hunt does not use it.

Kerala Mannaraśāla / other nāgarāja temples: Ananta and Vāsuki are the mūrtis; no separate short Sanskrit hello turned up (practice is milk, nūrūm pālum, Āyilyam). Tamil ஆதிசேஷன் / நாகர், Telugu ఆదిశేషుడు, Kannada ಆದಿಶೇಷ, Malayalam അനന്തൻ all carry the same nava-nāga line in Vignanam script-dumps; the long Ādiśeṣa stavam is the regional Śeṣa stotra, not a second short greeting.

## Uncertainties

- No Purāṇa / āgama / well-known bhāṣya verse-number for the nine-name anuṣṭubh. Bhaviṣya Uttara 36 lists overlapping nāgas for the vrata; it is not this stotra. Festival blogs that say “from the Purāṇa” are naming the *vrata*, not citing this line. Do not encode a fake Bhaviṣya number.
- Ananta and Śeṣa are often the same being (Ādiśeṣa). This verse lists them as two of nine; keep both names. पद्मनाभ here is the nāga Padma/Padmanābha, not Viṣṇu Padmanābha.
- (c) is medium because Nāga is a festival / protection smarana, not a daily school-yard hello like Gaṇeśa; the spoken act is often nāma or milk, and the 12-nāga paddhati dhyāna is the pūjā competitor. Among Sanskrit two-liners, this is still the one people know.
- Padaccheda and the supplied “I remember / salute” are from grammar and circulating arthas, not from a named padapāṭha.
- Subrahmaṇya overlap: Stotra Nidhi files this under Nāgadevatā and also prints it in a Subrahmaṇya pārāyaṇa book. It remains a nāga namāvali. Do not use the encoded Kārtikeya dhyāna.
- Which recension to encode later: verse 1 alone (this proposal; Gaṇeśa length) vs verse 1 + short phala (the complete tiny stotra). Do not mix in the longer Stotra Nidhi phala.
