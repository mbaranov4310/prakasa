# Candra

## Proposal

Devanagari:

दधिशङ्खतुषाराभं क्षीरोदार्णवसम्भवम् ।
नमामि शशिनं सोमं शम्भोर्मुकुटभूषणम् ॥

IAST:

dadhiśaṅkhatuṣārābhaṃ kṣīrodārṇavasambhavam |
namāmi śaśinaṃ somaṃ śambhormukuṭabhūṣaṇam ||

Padaccheda: not attested as a traditional pada-pāṭha; proposed from the usual anvaya in Indian-language teaching:

दधि-शङ्ख-तुषार-आभम् क्षीर-उद-अर्णव-सम्भवम् । नमामि शशिनम् सोमम् शम्भोः मुकुट-भूषणम् ॥

Word gloss:
- दधिशङ्खतुषाराभम् (दधि + शङ्ख + तुषार + आभम्) — of appearance like curd, a conch, and frost / snow
- क्षीरोदार्णवसम्भवम् (क्षीर + उद + अर्णव + सम्भवम्) — born from the ocean of milk
- नमामि — I bow
- शशिनम् — to Śaśin (the hare-marked Moon)
- सोमम् — to Soma here as the Moon, not the Vedic plant-deity
- शम्भोः — of Śambhu (Śiva)
- मुकुटभूषणम् — the crest-ornament

Translation: “I bow to Śaśin, Soma, who has the lustre of curd, conch, and frost, born from the ocean of milk, the ornament of Śambhu’s crown.”

Function: namaskāra / dhyānaśloka (pādas 1–2 and the names are dhyāna; नमामि is the bow). Same length as vakratuṇḍa. In paddhati the last two pādas are sometimes swapped for āvāhana.

Names searched: चन्द्र, चन्द्रमा, निशाकर, शशांक / शशाङ्क, इन्दु, शशी, सोम (Moon); Tamil சந்திரன் / திங்கள்; Telugu చంద్రుడు; Kannada ಚಂದ್ರ; Malayalam ചന്ദ്രൻ. Queries: श्लोक, प्रार्थना, ध्यान श्लोक, नमस्कार, स्तुति, स्तोत्रम्, दैनिक प्रार्थना, and Indian-language equivalents. Kept distinct from the Soma (plant / Vedic deity) job. No pan-Indian children’s school greeting on the vakratuṇḍa model turned up. This is the short navagraha namaskāra people actually extract and say.

## Claims

(a) exists: yes — verse 2 of the व्यास-विरचित नवग्रहस्तोत्रम्; also printed alone as Candra dhyāna / mantra
(b) traditionally used: yes — graha-japa dhyāna, gṛhapraveśa / kalasha / ādityādi-devatā āvāhana, nitya / dainandika navagraha collections
(c) standard short prayer: unclear — cannot find a kids’-school hello; this is the extracted one-śloka Candra namaskāra of the same Vyāsa set as Sūrya / Śani
Confidence in (c): low

## Variants

Do not splice.

- `सम्भवम्` (Wikisource, Stotra Nidhi) vs `संभवम्` (Sanskrit Documents) — same word
- `क्षीरोदार्णवसम्भवम्` (this recension) vs `क्षीरार्णव समुद्भवम्` (Vignanam lead line; पञ्चरत्न v. 1 `ददिशङ्ख तुषाराभं क्षीरार्णव समुद्भवम्`) — real circulating cousin; do not mix into the lead
- `मुकुट` (this recension) vs `मकुट` (Vignanam, पञ्चरत्न)
- `दधिशङ्ख` vs `दधिशंख` vs पञ्चरत्न `ददिशङ्ख`
- Spaced printing (`दधिशङ्ख तुषाराभं` … `शम्भो-र्मकुट भूषणम्`) vs one compound per pāda
- Paddhati last pādas: `ज्योत्स्नापतिं निशानाथं सोममावाहयाम्यहम्` (TransLiteral मण्डल āvāhana; do not splice into the namaskāra recension)
- Some prints insert `ॐ`; some split `दधिशंख तुषाराभं` for reading, not a different text

Do not mix the namaskāra pādas with the āvāhana pādas, and do not splice `समुद्भवम्` onto `क्षीरोदार्णव`.

## Other candidates

- `श्वेताम्बरः श्वेतवपुः किरीटी श्वेतद्युतिर्दण्डधरो द्विबाहुः । चन्द्रोऽमृतात्मा वरदः शशाङ्कः श्रेयांसि मह्यं प्रददातु देवः ॥` — iconographic dhyāna; Stotra Nidhi prints it *before* दधिशङ्ख in the bīja-japa; verse 1 of चन्द्रमस्स्तोत्रम् (Mantra-mahārṇava). Japa prints sometimes repeat `किरीटी` for `शशाङ्कः` and read `विदधातु`. Same length-class but denser; not the extracted navagraha hello. Why not lead.

## Uncertainties

- No Purāṇa / āgama verse-number for these four pādas. Traditional ascription is Vyāsa (stotra colophon). Pages that dump the verse into Skanda, Brahmāṇḍa, or Brahmavaivarta are mixing it with the pīḍāhara set, a kavaca, or a longer Candra stotra — do not encode a Purāṇa label. Wikisource’s Brahmāṇḍa pīḍāhara Candra verse is `रोहिणीशः सुधामूर्तिः…`, a different text.
- It lives both as verse 2 of a nine-verse navagraha stotra *and* as a standalone dhyāna / namaskāra (japa paddhati, āvāhana, पञ्चरत्न opening, Mantra-mahārṇava v. 2). Extraction is real; it is still not a morning-assembly verse like vakratuṇḍa.
- `सोम` in this śloka is the Moon (शशिन् / निशाकर). Do not fold it into the Soma-plant deity chapter.
- Vignanam’s nitya navagraha pūjā uses the collective `आदित्याय च सोमाय…`, not this dhyāna, for ṣoḍaśopacāra — same pattern as their Sūrya nitya pūjā preferring a different verse.
- Padaccheda and glosses are proposed from circulating Hindu anvaya / Hindi-Marathi meanings (दही, शंख, हिम/बर्फ; क्षीरसागर; शिव-मुकुट), not from a named bhāṣya.
- `तुषार` is frost / snow in the stotra glosses used here; one Marathi teaching page says dew-drop (दवबिंदू). Same white-cool image.
