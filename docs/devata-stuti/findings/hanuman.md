# Hanumān

## Proposal

Devanagari:

मनोजवं मारुततुल्यवेगं जितेन्द्रियं बुद्धिमतां वरिष्ठम् ।
वातात्मजं वानरयूथमुख्यं श्रीरामदूतं शरणं प्रपद्ये ॥

IAST:

manojavaṃ mārutatulyavegaṃ jitendriyaṃ buddhimatāṃ variṣṭham |
vātātmajaṃ vānarayūthamukhyaṃ śrīrāmadūtaṃ śaraṇaṃ prapadye ||

Padaccheda: not attested as a separate traditional sandhi-text; proposed from Gītā Press *Kalyāṇ* Hanumān Aṅka word-discussion, Śrī Sathya Sai Bālavikāsa gloss table, and Rāmarakṣā sārtha:

मनोजवं | मारुत-तुल्य-वेगं | जित-इन्द्रियं | बुद्धि-मतां | वरिष्ठम् ।
वात-आत्मजं | वानर-यूथ-मुख्यं | श्री-राम-दूतं | शरणं | प्रपद्ये ॥

Word gloss:
- मनस् — mind
- जवम् — speed
- मारुत — wind / Marut
- तुल्य — equal
- वेगम् — speed
- जित — conquered
- इन्द्रियम् — senses
- बुद्धि — buddhi / intellect
- मताम् — of those possessed of (gen. pl.)
- वरिष्ठम् — foremost, most excellent (acc. sg.)
- वात — Vāta / Vāyu
- आत्मजम् — son
- वानर — vānara
- यूथ — host
- मुख्यम् — chief
- श्री — Śrī
- राम — Rāma
- दूतम् — messenger
- शरणम् — refuge
- प्रपद्ये — I take refuge (1st sg. present, प्र-पद्)

Translation: “I take refuge in the messenger of Śrī Rāma — swift as thought, equal to the wind in speed, master of the senses, foremost among the wise, son of the Wind, chief of the vānara host.”

Function: namaskāra / śaraṇāgati (also used as dhyānaśloka before pūjā or parāyaṇa)

## Claims

(a) exists: yes — Rāmarakṣāstotram of Budha Kauśika, verse 33; printed and glossed as a standalone Hanumān verse in living Hindu books
(b) traditionally used: yes — nitya pūjā dhyāna, Āñjaneya ṣoḍaśopacāra, Bālavikāsa school prayer, Cālīsā / Sundarakāṇḍa frame (appended, not Vālmīki mūla)
(c) standard short prayer: yes — the common four-pāda Sanskrit hello; Cālīsā is more popular as daily recitation but too long for this yardstick
Confidence in (c): high (among short Sanskrit greetings; regional competitors exist — see Other candidates)

## Variants

- Closing pāda: `श्रीरामदूतं शरणं प्रपद्ये` (Rāmarakṣā 33, Bālavikāsa, Gītā Press lead) vs `श्रीरामदूतं शिरसा नमामि` (nitya pūjā / namaskāra / Āñjaneya dhyāna sets). Gītā Press: “'शरणं प्रपद्ये' के स्थानपर कहीं-कहीं 'शिरसा नमामि' पाठ है।” Do not splice.
- Orthography, not recension: `मारुततुल्यवेगं` vs spaced `मारुत तुल्य वेगं`; `श्रीरामदूतं` vs `श्री रामदूतं`; `वरिष्ठम्` vs careless `वरिष्टम्`.
- Not a variant of this verse: blogs that call it Vālmīki Sundarakāṇḍa. It is not a Vālmīki mūla; it is used *before* Sundarakāṇḍa / Cālīsā parāyaṇa.

## Other candidates

- `बुद्धिर्बलं यशो धैर्यं निर्भयत्वमरोगता । अजाड्यं वाक्पटुत्वं च हनुमत्स्मरणाद्भवेत् ॥` — equally common one-anuṣṭubh, especially as a student’s smarana-phala; function is “by remembering Hanumān these eight arise,” not a greeting. Gītā Press cites Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa, Manoharakāṇḍa 13.16 with a different pāda (`सुदाढ्यं वाक्स्फुरत्वं च`); Sanātana Sansthā cites Parāśara Saṃhitā, paṭala 76, śloka 15. Two mūla-claims; verse-number not re-checked here against a clean printed Parāśara Saṃhitā.
- `अतुलितबलधामं हेमशैलाभदेहं दनुजवनकृशानुं ज्ञानिनामग्रगण्यम् । सकलगुणनिधानं वानराणामधीशं रघुपतिप्रियभक्तं वातजातं नमामि ॥` — standard dhyāna before Cālīsā / Sundarakāṇḍa; denser śārdūlavikrīḍita. Variants: `हेमशैलाभ` / `स्वर्णशैलाभ`; `वातजातं` / `वातात्मजं`. Lead if a longer iconographic dhyāna is wanted.
- `आञ्जनेयमतिपाटलाननं काञ्चनाद्रिकमनीयविग्रहम् । पारिजाततरुमूलवासिनं भावयामि पवमाननन्दनम् ॥` — common South-Indian Āñjaneya dhyāna (Tamil / Telugu / Kerala pūjā sets); not the pan-Indian short hello.
- `गोष्पदीकृतवारीशं मशकीकृतराक्षसम् । रामायणमहामालारत्नं वन्देऽनिलात्मजम् ॥` — one anuṣṭubh namaskāra in Hanumannamaskāra collections; less independently memorized than मनोजवं.
- `यत्र यत्र रघुनाथकीर्तनं तत्र तत्र कृतमस्तकाञ्जलिम् । बाष्पवारिपरिपूर्णलोचनं मारुतिं नमत राक्षसान्तकम् ॥` — famous; more a kīrtana-phala / namaskāra in a set than the daily four-pāda hello.
- `उल्लङ्घ्य सिन्धोः सलिलं सलीलं यः शोकवह्निं जनकात्मजायाः । आदाय तेनैव ददाह लङ्कां नमामि तं प्राञ्जलिराञ्जनेयम् ॥` — beloved dhyāna; longer metre; usually in a cluster, not the single greeting.
- Āñjaneya trikāla vandanam (`प्रातः स्मरामि हनुमन्तमनन्तवीर्यं…` and two companions) — Gītā Press *Kalyāṇ* Hanumān Aṅka / paddhati; three verses, not one hello.
- `ॐ आञ्जनेयाय विद्महे वायुपुत्राय धीमहि । तन्नो हनुमत् प्रचोदयात् ॥` — Gāyatrī; mantra, not a śloka-greeting.
- Hanumān Cālīsā (Tulasīdāsa) — *the* North-Indian daily recitation; too long for this yardstick. Many prints append मनोजवं after it; that does not make the verse part of the Cālīsā.
- Samartha Rāmadāsa, Marathi Māruti stotra (`भीमरूपी महारुद्रा…`) and the children’s `सुखकर्ता दुःखहर्ता` — Maharashtra’s living hello; vernacular and longer than 1–2 ślokas.

## Uncertainties

- No Purāṇa / āgama verse-number for मनोजवं. Strong as Rāmarakṣāstotram 33 and as a living dhyāna / school verse, not as a pinned Vālmīki or Purāṇa mūla.
- Popular sites call it “Sundarakāṇḍa dhyāna from Vālmīki.” That is parāyaṇa practice, not a Vālmīki citation. Do not encode a fake Sundarakāṇḍa number.
- Which closing to encode: `शरणं प्रपद्ये` (this proposal; matches the Kārtikeya śaraṇāgati shape) vs `शिरसा नमामि` (explicit namaskāra in paddhati). Both are traditional; pick one, do not mix.
- बुद्धिर्बलं is the main rival short verse; its own mūla is disputed (Ānanda Rāmāyaṇa vs Parāśara Saṃhitā) and its function is phala, not namaskāra.
- Regional “the” prayer is often not Sanskrit: Cālīsā (North), Rāmadāsa Māruti (Maharashtra), Āñjaneya dhyāna cluster (South). This proposal is the short Sanskrit greeting that still travels across those regions.
