# aghamarsana

## complement
from: ag-drupadat-1
to: ag-muncatu
judgment: obvious
note: Ablative. The post is what one is released from.

## complement
from: ag-drupadat-2
to: ag-mumucanah
judgment: obvious
note: Ablative. The post is what he has been released from.

## describes
from: ag-mumucanah
judgment: unsure
why: No nominative in the chapter agrees with mumucānaḥ; it describes an implied speaker.

## describes
from: ag-svinnah
judgment: unsure
why: No nominative in its sentence agrees with svinnaḥ; it describes an implied person.

## complement
from: ag-malat
judgment: unsure
why: malāt can go with snātvī (“bathed from dirt”) or with an unstated verb of release that the simile implies.

## complement
from: ag-pavitrena
to: ag-putam
judgment: obvious
note: Instrumental. The strainer is what it is purified with.

## describes
from: ag-putam
to: ag-ajyam
judgment: obvious
note: pūtam matches ājyam in case, whether that case is nominative or accusative. ājyam is the only word in the chapter it can agree with.

## subject
from: ag-sundhantu
to: ag-apah
judgment: unsure
why: āpaḥ is nominative or vocative, and the chapter's phrase reads it as the vocative “O waters.”

## complement
from: ag-me
to: ag-manasah
judgment: obvious
note: Genitive. The mind is what belongs to me.

## complement
from: ag-manasah
judgment: unsure
why: As a genitive manasaḥ has nothing in the line to belong to, and as an ablative it would go with śundhantu.
