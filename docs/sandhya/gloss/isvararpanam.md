# isvararpanam

## ā
ids: ia-a
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: A preposition with the ablative, “from / as far as.”
meaning: from / as far as

## brahma
ids: ia-brahma
kind: noun
judgment: unsure
why: brahma is the first member of brahmalokād. The ending sits on lokād.
meaning: Brahman

## lokād
ids: ia-lokad
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: brahmaloka
gender: masculine
case: ablative
number: singular
note: lokāt before a vowel.
meaning: from the world

## aśeṣād
ids: ia-asesad
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: aśeṣa
gender: masculine
case: ablative
number: singular
root: śiṣ
note: aśeṣāt before a vowel. -āt is also neuter, and it agrees with brahmalokād.
meaning: without remainder

## ālokāloka
ids: ia-alokaloka
kind: noun
judgment: unsure
why: ālokāloka is the first member of ālokālokaparvatām. The ending sits on parvatām.
meaning: Ālokāloka

## parvatām
ids: ia-parvatam
kind: noun
judgment: unsure
why: parvata is a masculine -a stem, and parvatām is none of its regular forms. The ā … ablative pattern expects parvatāt, and the accusative would be parvatam.
meaning: mountain

## ye
ids: ia-ye
kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: yad
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: plural
note: ye is also feminine or neuter dual. santi is plural, so this is the masculine plural.
meaning: who

## santi
ids: ia-santi
kind: verb
judgment: obvious
root: as
tense: present

person: 3rd
number: plural
meaning: exist

## brāhmaṇa
ids: ia-brahmana
kind: noun
judgment: unsure
why: The chapter writes short brāhmaṇa joined to devās, which is a compound front member with no ending, but the meaning “brāhmaṇas” reads it as nominative plural brāhmaṇāḥ.
meaning: brāhmaṇas

## devās
ids: ia-devah
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: deva
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: plural
note: devāḥ before t. The chapter writes brāhmaṇa as its front member.
meaning: gods

## tebhyo
ids: ia-tebhyo
kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: tad
gender: masculine
case: dative
number: plural
note: tebhyaḥ before a nasal. -bhyaḥ also forms the ablative plural, but namaḥ takes the dative. The form is also neuter; it points back to ye and devāḥ.
meaning: to them
