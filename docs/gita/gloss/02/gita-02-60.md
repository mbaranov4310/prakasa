# gita-02-60

## yatataḥ
ids: gita-02-60-w1
kind: participle
judgment: obvious
root: yat
participle: present
gender: masculine
case: genitive
number: singular
note: Present participle of yat, “to strive.” The ending -ataḥ is genitive or ablative. It agrees with puruṣasya, so it is genitive. Written yatato before hi.
meaning: of one who is striving

## hi
ids: gita-02-60-w2
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: Written hy before api.
meaning: for

## api
ids: gita-02-60-w3
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: The a joins hi.
meaning: even

## kaunteya
ids: gita-02-60-w4
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: kaunteya
gender: masculine
case: vocative
number: singular
note: A patronymic, “son of Kuntī,” a name of Arjuna.
meaning: son of Kuntī

## puruṣasya
ids: gita-02-60-w5
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: puruṣa
gender: masculine
case: genitive
number: singular
meaning: of a man

## vipaścitaḥ
ids: gita-02-60-w6
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: vipaścit
gender: masculine
case: genitive
number: singular
note: “One who discerns,” from vi-paś, “to see clearly.” vipaścitaḥ is genitive or ablative. It agrees with puruṣasya, so it is genitive.
meaning: who discerns

## indriyāṇi
ids: gita-02-60-w7
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: indriya
gender: neuter
case: nominative
number: plural
note: The neuter plural -āṇi is nominative or accusative. The senses are what carry the mind off, so it is nominative.
meaning: the senses

## pramāthīni
ids: gita-02-60-w8
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: pramāthin
gender: neuter
case: nominative
number: plural
note: pra-math, “to churn,” with the suffix -in. Neuter plural to agree with indriyāṇi. The nominative plural of a neuter stem in -in is -īni.
meaning: that churn him

number: plural

## prasabham
ids: gita-02-60-w10
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious

## manaḥ
ids: gita-02-60-w11
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: manas
gender: neuter
case: accusative
number: singular
root: man
note: The neuter manaḥ is nominative or accusative. It is what the senses carry off, so it is accusative.
