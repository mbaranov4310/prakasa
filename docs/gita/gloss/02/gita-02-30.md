# gita-02-30

## dehī
ids: gita-02-30-w1
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: dehin
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
note: A stem in -in, “one who has a body.” The nominative singular ends in -ī. It stands as a noun.
meaning: the embodied one

## nityam
ids: gita-02-30-w2
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: The neuter accusative singular of nitya, used as an adverb. Written nityam before avadhyaḥ.
meaning: always

## avadhyaḥ
ids: gita-02-30-w3
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: avadhya
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
root: han
note: a- (“not”) with vadhya, the potential participle in -ya of han. vadh is the form han takes here. Written avadhyo before the a of ayam, and that a is dropped. There is no finite verb. “Is” is understood.
meaning: beyond killing

## ayam
ids: gita-02-30-w4
kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: idam
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
note: Its initial a is dropped after avadhyaḥ.
meaning: he

## tasmāt
ids: gita-02-30-w8
kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: tad
gender: neuter
case: ablative
number: singular
note: The ablative singular is the same in masculine and neuter. It points back to the self just described, so it is neuter. Written tasmāt before sarvāṇi.
meaning: therefore

## sarvāṇi
ids: gita-02-30-w9
kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: sarva
gender: neuter
case: accusative
number: plural
note: The neuter plural -āṇi is nominative or accusative. It agrees with bhūtāni, what the grief is about, so it is accusative.
meaning: all

## bhūtāni
ids: gita-02-30-w10
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: bhūta
gender: neuter
case: accusative
number: plural
root: bhū
note: bhūta is the past participle of bhū, used as a noun. The neuter plural is what you would grieve for, so it is accusative.
meaning: beings
