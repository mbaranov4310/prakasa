# gita-02-59

## viṣayāḥ
ids: gita-02-59-w1
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: viṣaya
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: plural
note: The visarga drops before vinivartante. Śaṅkara takes the word as the senses, which go by the names of their objects.
meaning: the objects (the senses that bear their names)

## vinivartante
ids: gita-02-59-w2
kind: verb
judgment: obvious
root: vṛt
prefix: vi-ni
tense: present
voice: ātmanepada
person: 3rd
number: plural
meaning: turn back

## nirāhārasya
ids: gita-02-59-w3
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: nirāhāra
gender: masculine
case: genitive
number: singular
compound: bahuvrīhi
note: nis- (“without”) with āhāra, “taking in”: one who does not take the objects in. It agrees with dehinaḥ. The pada is not hyphenated.
meaning: of one who does not take them in

## dehinaḥ
ids: gita-02-59-w4
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: dehin
gender: masculine
case: genitive
number: singular
note: deha with the suffix -in, “having a body.” dehinaḥ is genitive or ablative singular, and also nominative plural. It agrees with nirāhārasya, so it is genitive singular.
meaning: of the embodied

## rasa-varjam
ids: gita-02-59-w5
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: rasa, “taste,” with varjam, “excluding”: the neuter used as an adverb, “except the taste.” Śaṅkara takes rasa as the relish for objects.
meaning: except the taste (the relish for them)

## rasaḥ
ids: gita-02-59-w6
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: rasa
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
root: ras
note: Written raso before the a of api, and that a is dropped.
meaning: the taste

## api
ids: gita-02-59-w7
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: The a is dropped after rasaḥ, and the i joins asya: pyasya.
meaning: even

## asya
ids: gita-02-59-w8
kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: idam
gender: masculine
case: genitive
number: singular
note: The a joins the i of api.
meaning: his

## param
ids: gita-02-59-w9
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: para
gender: neuter
case: accusative
number: singular
note: The adjective stands as a noun, the highest reality. The neuter -am is nominative or accusative. It is what he has seen, so it is accusative.
meaning: the highest

## dṛṣṭvā
ids: gita-02-59-w10
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
root: dṛś
note: An absolutive in -tvā.
meaning: having seen

## nivartate
ids: gita-02-59-w11
kind: verb
judgment: obvious
root: vṛt
prefix: ni
tense: present
voice: ātmanepada
person: 3rd
number: singular
