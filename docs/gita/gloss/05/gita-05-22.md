## ye

kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: yad
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: plural
meaning: which

## hi
ids: gita-05-22-w2
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: Śaṅkara reads hi as yasmāt, “because.”
meaning: for

## saṃsparśa-jāḥ
ids: gita-05-22-w3
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: saṃsparśa-ja
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: plural
root: jan
compound: tatpuruṣa
note: Born (ja) of the contact (saṃsparśa) of sense and object. The visarga drops before bhogāḥ.
meaning: born of contact (of sense and object)

## bhogāḥ
ids: gita-05-22-w4
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: bhoga
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: plural
root: bhuj
note: The visarga drops before duḥkha-yonayaḥ.
meaning: enjoyments

## duḥkha-yonayaḥ
ids: gita-05-22-w5
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: duḥkha-yoni
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: plural
compound: tatpuruṣa
note: “Sources (yoni) of pain.” The ending -ayaḥ is shared by masculine and feminine; it agrees with bhogāḥ, so it is masculine. Written duḥkhayonaya before eva.
meaning: sources of pain

## eva
ids: gita-05-22-w6
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: Śaṅkara reads eva as covering the next world as well as this one.
meaning: only (and so in the next world as well)

## ādya-anta-vantaḥ
ids: gita-05-22-w8
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: ādya-anta-vat
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: plural
compound: bahuvrīhi
note: Having a beginning and an end. ādi is written ādya before anta. Śaṅkara takes the beginning as the meeting of sense and object, and the end as their parting.
meaning: having a beginning and an end (the meeting of sense and object, and their parting)
