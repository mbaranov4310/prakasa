# gita-03-09

## yajña-arthāt
ids: gita-03-09-w1
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: yajña-artha
gender: neuter
case: ablative
number: singular
compound: tatpuruṣa
note: For the sake of sacrifice. It agrees with karmaṇaḥ, so it is neuter. Written joined to karmaṇaḥ. Śaṅkara takes yajña as the Lord.
meaning: done for sacrifice (which is the Lord)

## karmaṇaḥ
ids: gita-03-09-w2
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: karman
gender: neuter
case: ablative
number: singular
root: kṛ
note: The genitive and the ablative of karman are both karmaṇaḥ. anyatra takes the ablative, “other than action,” so it is ablative. Written karmaṇo' before anyatra.
meaning: than action

## anyatra
ids: gita-03-09-w3
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
note: The initial a is dropped after o, marked by the avagraha.
meaning: other than

## lokaḥ
ids: gita-03-09-w4
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: loka
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
note: Written loko' before ayam. Śaṅkara takes this world as the man who is qualified to act.
meaning: the world

## ayam
ids: gita-03-09-w5
kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: idam
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
note: The initial a is dropped after o, marked by the avagraha. Written yaṃ.
meaning: this

## karma-bandhanaḥ
ids: gita-03-09-w6
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: karma-bandhana
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
compound: bahuvrīhi
note: “Action is whose bond.” bandhana as a noun is neuter. The masculine -aḥ agrees with lokaḥ, so this is the bahuvrīhi. Śaṅkara glosses it that way.
meaning: has action for its bond

## tat-artham
ids: gita-03-09-w7
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: tat-artha
gender: neuter
case: accusative
number: singular
compound: tatpuruṣa
note: The neuter -am is nominative or accusative. It agrees with karma, what he is told to perform, so it is accusative. For that end, the same “for the sake of” as yajñārthāt. Written tadarthaṃ. Śaṅkara glosses yajñārtham.
meaning: for that end

## karma
ids: gita-03-09-w8
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: karman
gender: neuter
case: accusative
number: singular
root: kṛ
note: The neuter karma is nominative or accusative. It is what he is told to perform, so it is accusative.
meaning: action

## mukta-saṅgaḥ
ids: gita-03-09-w10
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: mukta-saṅga
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
compound: bahuvrīhi
note: “Whose attachment is released.” mukta is the past participle of muc. The nominative agrees with the unstated “you” of the command, not with the vocative. Śaṅkara glosses one who has given up attachment to the fruit, and adds san.
meaning: free of attachment (to the fruit)
