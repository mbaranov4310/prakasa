# gita-04-20

## tyaktvā
ids: gita-04-20-w1
kind: indeclinable
judgment: obvious
root: tyaj
note: Absolutive of tyaj.
meaning: having given up

## karma-phala-āsaṅgam
ids: gita-04-20-w2
kind: noun
judgment: obvious
stem: karma-phala-āsaṅga
gender: masculine
case: accusative
number: singular
root: sañj
prefix: ā
compound: tatpuruṣa
note: The long ā in karma-phalāsaṅgam is phala meeting āsaṅga, attachment, from ā-sañj. Śaṅkara reads the word as two things given up: the claim of being the doer in action, and attachment to its fruit. It is what he gives up, so it is accusative.
meaning: the claim of being the doer in action, and attachment to its fruit

## nitya-tṛptaḥ
ids: gita-04-20-w3
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: nitya-tṛpta
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
root: tṛp
compound: tatpuruṣa
note: tṛpta is the past participle of tṛp. Śaṅkara: with no longing for objects. It agrees with saḥ. Written nityatṛpto before nirāśrayaḥ.
meaning: always content

## nirāśrayaḥ
ids: gita-04-20-w4
kind: adjective
judgment: obvious
stem: nir-āśraya
gender: masculine
case: nominative
number: singular
compound: bahuvrīhi
note: Without a support. āśraya is from ā-śri. Śaṅkara: nothing one leans on in order to reach a human end. It agrees with saḥ.
meaning: with nothing he leans on

## kiṃcit
ids: gita-04-20-w10
kind: pronoun
judgment: obvious
stem: kim
gender: neuter
case: accusative
number: singular
note: kim with the particle cit, "anything." The neuter form is nominative or accusative. It is what he does not do, so it is accusative. Joined with karoti.
meaning: anything

person: 3rd
number: singular
note: Śaṅkara: what he does is non-action, because he sees the self as without action.
meaning: does
